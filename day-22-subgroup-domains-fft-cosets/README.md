# Day 22: Subgroup Domain Structures for FFTs & Coset Domains

## 1. Overview & Mathematical Objective
In modern ZK-SNARK proving systems (PLONK, Groth16, STARKs), constraint enforcement is transformed from coefficient evaluations into polynomial division identities over cyclic evaluation domains:

$$C(X) = A(X) \cdot B(X) - C(X) = Q(X) \cdot Z_H(X)$$

To evaluate and interpolate polynomials of degree $n-1$ in $O(n \log n)$ time instead of $O(n^2)$, provers execute Number Theoretic Transforms (NTTs/FFTs) over cyclic subgroups $H = \langle \omega \rangle$ generated by primitive $n$-th roots of unity ($\omega^n \equiv 1 \pmod p$).

---

## 2. Core Concepts

### A. Divide-and-Conquer: Even-Odd Decomposition
A polynomial $P(X)$ of degree $n-1$ is split into even and odd degree components:

$$P(X) = P_{\text{even}}(X^2) + X \cdot P_{\text{odd}}(X^2)$$

Where:
* $P_{\text{even}}(Y) = a_0 + a_2 Y + a_4 Y^2 + \dots$
* $P_{\text{odd}}(Y) = a_1 + a_3 Y + a_5 Y^2 + \dots$

### B. The Halving Lemma (Squaring Roots of Unity)
Squaring every element in $H = \{\omega^0, \omega^1, \dots, \omega^{n-1}\}$ folds the domain in half:

$$(\omega^{i + n/2})^2 = (-\omega^i)^2 = \omega^{2i}$$

The squared set $H^2$ forms a subgroup of size $n/2$ generated by $\omega^2$.

### C. Butterfly Optimization
By pairing symmetric roots $\omega^i$ and $\omega^{i + n/2} = -\omega^i$:
* $P(\omega^i) = P_{\text{even}}(\omega^{2i}) + \omega^i \cdot P_{\text{odd}}(\omega^{2i})$
* $P(\omega^{i + n/2}) = P_{\text{even}}(\omega^{2i}) - \omega^i \cdot P_{\text{odd}}(\omega^{2i})$

Evaluating both points requires only one evaluation of $P_{\text{even}}$ and $P_{\text{odd}}$.

---

## 3. Coset Shift Domains ($g \cdot H$) & Zero-Knowledge

A coset $g \cdot H$ is formed by shifting every element of $H$ by an offset $g \notin H$:

$$g \cdot H = \{ g \cdot 1, g \cdot \omega, g \cdot \omega^2, \dots, g \cdot \omega^{n-1} \}$$

### Why Cosets are Necessary:
1. **Preventing Division by Zero (Vanishing Singularity):**
   The quotient polynomial denominator $Z_H(X) = X^n - 1$ vanishes to $0$ on domain $H$. Evaluating over shifted domain $g \cdot H$ prevents singularities:
   
   $$Z_H(g \cdot \omega^i) = (g \cdot \omega^i)^n - 1 = g^n \cdot (\omega^n)^i - 1 = g^n - 1 \neq 0 \pmod p \quad (\text{since } g \notin H)$$

2. **Zero-Knowledge Masking:** 
   Evaluating constraints on shifted cosets blinds execution trace values from leaking secrets.

3. **Inversion Optimization:**
   Because $Z_H(X) \equiv g^n - 1$ is constant everywhere on $g \cdot H$, computing $Q(X)$ requires only **1 single modular inversion** $(g^n - 1)^{-1} \pmod p$ across all $n$ evaluation points.

---

## 4. Security Vulnerabilities & Attack Vectors

### 1. Coset Domain Collision ($g \in H$)
* **Mechanism:** If $g \in H$ (or $g^n \equiv 1 \pmod p$), the coset $g \cdot H$ collapses back into $H$.
* **Impact:** The denominator $g^n - 1 \equiv 0$, causing division-by-zero crashes or malicious provers exploiting undefined states to forge valid proofs for invalid execution traces.

### 2. FFT Domain Aliasing (Degree-Bound Wrap-Around)
* **Mechanism:** Multiplying two degree $(n-1)$ polynomials yields a product of degree $2n-2$. If multiplication is evaluated over an FFT domain of size $n$, higher powers wrap around modulo $X^n - 1$.
* **Impact:** Attackers inject high-degree malformed terms that satisfy constraints at domain evaluation points while violating circuit logic outside the domain.

---

## 5. ZK Circuit Auditor Checklist

- [ ] **Coset Multiplier Non-Membership:** Assert $g \notin H$ and $g^n \not\equiv 1 \pmod p$ to ensure disjoint evaluation sets.
- [ ] **Extended Domain Sizing:** Ensure FFT domains for polynomial multiplication are expanded to size $\ge \text{deg}(A) + \text{deg}(B) + 1$ (e.g., $2n$ or $4n$) to eliminate aliasing.
- [ ] **Prover-Verifier Domain Alignment:** Verify that circuit compilers, proving engines, and on-chain verification contracts share identical domain size $n$, root $\omega$, and shift generator $g$.
